# Research visuals

Three homepage research visuals communicate the owner’s research directions. They explain research directions, not fitted experimental models, measured recordings, or clinical tools. Scope notes stay in these maintenance docs rather than decorative UI disclaimers.

## Brain network modeling

The default view uses MRI-derived pial, cerebellar and brainstem meshes from Anderson Winkler’s [Brain for Blender / Brainder](https://brainder.org/research/brain-for-blender/), licensed CC BY-SA 3.0. `assets/brain-mesh.js` renders simplified geometry with native WebGL: shaded cortical relief, surface wire edges, moving activation fronts, sparse points and a slowly changing three-quarter view. Geometry attribution, download links and derivative licensing are in `assets/brain/README.md`, linked from the public footer. `scripts/build-brain-mesh.py` reconstructs the display assets from MZ3 archives.

A 28-second automatic cycle holds the cortical network, crossfades into a transparent cortex with hippocampal and amygdala meshes, then returns to the surface. The view buttons were removed. A dedicated animation clock pauses with the main playback and does not progress while another topic is active. Leader lines locate cortical lobes, cerebellum and brainstem; the hypothalamus is explicitly labeled as an approximate region, not an isolated segmented mesh. Labels are orientation aids, not quantitative parcellation. The model’s true folded shape replaces the rejected hand-drawn anatomical outline. A static SVG projection from the same data supports loading, WebGL failure and JavaScript-disabled viewing.

Surface topology is not a measured neuronal connectome. Activity and local perturbation propagation are decorative procedural signals, not recorded neural data or validated functional predictions. Promotional circuit-brain imagery, including [Baylor College of Medicine’s cerebellar AI article](https://www.bcm.edu/news/neuroscientists-develop-ai-tool-to-unlock-cerebellums-secrets), informed the luminous wire treatment; no promotional stock image is copied into the site.

## Continuous conscious states

The state scene shares the network scene’s forest-green lighting, mint signals and restrained amber attention channel. `assets/state-experience.js` combines an Apple Watch display and an EEG electrode cap, with animated acquisition traces feeding one shared state graph. `assets/watch-display.js` composites original screen content under Apple’s official Series 11 product artwork, with a fixed front-facing device, preserved proportions and no rotating or hand-drawn case. The screen prioritizes the current state, labels the two compact feature histories and shows a qualitative two-hour outlook. Its time, values and future state share the main graph’s models and current plan. Product artwork provenance and processing are documented in `assets/devices/README.md`; the footer links its credit page. These are not live hardware connections. The watch does not record EEG or directly measure attention; the display communicates the proposed multimodal research direction. A visible “样例日程” label identifies the constructed day.

The graph spans a sample day into the next morning. Historical state features retain separate arousal and attention trajectories with NREM/REM, drowsiness, focus, wandering attention and fog. A cursor divides sample history from future scenarios. Future curves are dashed, feature uncertainty bands, and include a gray comparison against a later schedule (coffee 17:00, dinner 21:00, sleep 00:00). State bands are derived from the same signal or historical schedule used by the curves.

Three labeled, keyboard-accessible selectors adjust the final coffee time, dinner time and intended bedtime. `forecastSignal` uses qualitative heuristic response shapes, not validated biological parameters, calibrated probability intervals or personalized forecasts. Changes apply only after the selected origin and remain continuous there. No dose recommendations, clinical scores or treatment claims are made. The model is deliberately not represented as a delivered product or a clinical predictor. Playback advances 0.035 sample hours per second; the current-time slider holds the timeline during editing. Planning changes remain available while paused.

Reference context: [Apple Watch sleep tracking](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/108906) supports describing sleep-related wearable information; [Apple’s sleep-stage estimation paper](https://www.apple.com/health/pdf/Estimating_Sleep_Stages_from_Apple_Watch_Oct_2025.pdf) distinguishes wearable estimates from EEG-based reference assessments. [NHLBI healthy sleep habits](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-deprivation/healthy-sleep-habits) supports general schedule considerations around caffeine and meals. These references do not validate this site’s illustrative prediction formula.

## Brain–behavior alignment

A seeded, stochastic model switches between exploration, sniffing, grooming, pausing and short runs. Speed and angular velocity have inertia; head movements are scheduled independently; the tail is a constrained following chain. Simulation uses fixed 1/60-second updates independent of display rate. The mouse is rendered with an oblique orthographic camera on a raised arena floor. `mouse-renderer.js` uses cached native WebGL geometry, interpolated surface normals and per-pixel lighting for a low, tapered body, independently turning head, thin tilted ears, glossy eyes, delicate feet and toes, and a smooth segmented tail. Ground shadows and paw height follow the existing behavior clock. A depth buffer gives the mouse and beveled objects consistent occlusion; the former Canvas mesh renderer remains a fallback when WebGL is unavailable. A flatter camera on wide desktop viewports expands the arena across the available screen; the mobile composition stays compact. The physical and neural models are unchanged by the camera.

The behavior arena fills the main visual field. Large floating instruments overlap its edges: a circular optical window at the upper right (up to 356px), an event raster at the upper left (up to 350 × 172px), and a shared timeline near the lower edge (up to 610 × 186px). Labels and signal rows scale with the panels. Dark translucent surfaces, luminous traces and shadows keep these instruments legible over the arena. Mobile uses a 166px optical window, a 120px-tall raster and a full-width 136px-tall timeline around the arena. In this scene the duplicate hero name is hidden and the viewport moves upward; the navigation retains the personal brand and the canvas retains its “行为追踪” title.

Three raised objects (a column, block and low platform) are defined once in `BehaviorModel.obstacles` and used by rendering and collision handling. The animal selects object-approach targets, steers around obstacles and pauses to investigate them. Circular footprint clearance conservatively bounds the square block. Fixed-step collision resolution preserves the animal’s footprint, while object placement leaves enough room inside arena walls. Sniff-related neural tuning uses the same resulting behavior state and clock.

Behavior-dependent activity modulates probabilistic neural-event rates, with independent baseline activity. Calcium fluorescence uses a rapid rise and slower exponential decay after each modeled event. The optical window, raster, speed trace and fluorescence trace share exactly the same simulation time and an 8-second history. This demonstrates alignment, not a one-to-one causal mapping or spike inference from fluorescence. The optical texture is drawn, not captured microscopy. The imaging field is a circular cranial window enclosing a bilateral dorsal mouse-cortex silhouette. Fluorescence domains, fine tissue texture and branching vessels are clipped to that silhouette, with a visible midline and a narrow outer window rim. The user’s reference images guided the shape. Each activity domain keeps its original modeled cell’s event and fluorescence clock; no separate cosmetic activity timer is introduced. Hit testing initializes under an identity canvas transform, and candidate sampling is bounded.

Scientific context: [Janelia GENIE publications](https://www.janelia.org/project-team/genie/publication-list) describe calcium-indicator kinetics relative to electrical activity; [Janelia’s GENIE proposal](https://www.janelia.org/sites/default/files/GENIE_proposal_April_30_2014.pdf) explains calcium fluorescence as a proxy for electrical activity; [Allen Institute for Neural Dynamics](https://alleninstitute.org/neural-dynamics) describes connecting brain-wide activity, behavior and models.

## Validation

`node --test tests/research-models.test.cjs` checks stop/start variation, arena bounds, independent head motion, tail continuity, bounded histories, frame-rate independence, delayed calcium peaks and decay, state coverage, continuity at state transitions, feature separation and state-dependent variability. Additional multi-seed trajectories verify obstacle clearance, arena bounds, continuous movement and object investigation. The locomotor-tuning test disables approach targets to isolate sustained movement from investigation transitions.

UI: all animations stop when paused, offscreen or the page is hidden; reduced-motion starts paused. Scrubbing and topic changes still work while paused. The static SVG brain remains visible if JavaScript or Canvas is unavailable. No outside animation library is required. Unit checks additionally verify continuous automatic brain transitions and planning-curve continuity, boundedness and separation from the past.
